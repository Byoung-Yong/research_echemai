Research Note 29 · 24 November 2026

Can a pretrained potential predict lithium electrode potentials across different electrolytes?

A transferable electrolyte potential and targeted first-principles fine-tuning make explicit-solvent calculations of lithium electrode potentials more computationally accessible.

Editorial note by EchemAI · Literature window: 30 October–5 November 2026

In brief

QuestionCan a reusable machine-learning model capture the electrolyte dependence of lithium metal potential without training a separate potential from scratch?
ResultThe authors report ab initio-level accuracy using about 200 additional DFT calculations per electrolyte system.
Why it mattersThe approach connects the local coordination of Li⁺ with a measurable electrochemical thermodynamic quantity.
Fine-Tuning Universal Machine Learning Potentials Enables Efficient Computation of Li Electrode Potentials in Battery Electrolytes
Feng Wang, Yu-Hang Tang, Yi-Min Wei and Jun Cheng · Journal of the American Chemical Society · Published online 31 October 2026
DOI: 10.1021/jacs.6c12519

The electrochemical problem

Lithium electrode potential depends on the free-energy balance between metal and solvated Li⁺. Small cluster calculations omit many solvent interactions, whereas extensive ab initio molecular dynamics can be too costly to sample the relevant liquid configurations.

What the researchers did

Wang and colleagues combined a general-purpose machine-learning potential for electrolyte systems with fine-tuning based on targeted density functional theory calculations. The workflow was used to compute lithium electrode potentials with an explicit description of the surrounding electrolyte.

What they found

The publisher abstract reports first-principles-level accuracy after roughly 200 additional DFT calculations for each electrolyte studied. It also reports a relationship between the local solvation environment of lithium ions and changes in predicted electrode potential.

Why this matters

Transfer learning may reduce the cost of evaluating electrolyte-dependent electrode thermodynamics. It also offers a way to connect computed voltage shifts to local solution structure, rather than treating the potential as an isolated regression target.

One important caution

Agreement with ab initio reference calculations is a computational validation, not necessarily agreement with independent measurements. The detailed error distribution, electrolyte coverage and computational cost were unavailable in the reviewed abstract; the reported training-data requirement should not be generalized to arbitrary electrolyte chemistries.

Primary source

Feng Wang, Yu-Hang Tang, Yi-Min Wei and Jun Cheng. Fine-Tuning Universal Machine Learning Potentials Enables Efficient Computation of Li Electrode Potentials in Battery Electrolytes. Journal of the American Chemical Society 148, 47908–47919 (2026). DOI · PubMed.
