Research Note 32 · 24 November 2026

Can machine-learning-assisted tomography reveal local electrochemical activity inside a composite electrode?

Interrupted-operando X-ray nano-holotomography combines learned phase segmentation with particle-scale strain and transport analysis to reveal heterogeneous battery-electrode behavior.

Editorial note by EchemAI · Literature window: 20–24 November 2026 (partial interval)

In brief

QuestionCan local structure and transport explain why neighboring graphite particles behave differently during electrode formation?
ResultThe workflow tracked 56 of 66 selected graphite particles during formation and identified fast ion-transport pathways as an important factor in local reaction heterogeneity.
Why it mattersThe study illustrates how machine learning can turn high-dimensional experimental images into quantitative electrochemical evidence.
Four-dimensional X-ray nano-holotomography reveals multiscale chemomechanics in composite graphite–silicon battery electrodes
Victor Vanpeene, Olga Stamati, Francois Cadiou, Quentin Jacquet, Julie Villanova and Sandrine Lyonnard · Nature Nanotechnology · Published online 20 November 2026
DOI: 10.1038/s41565-026-02327-6

The electrochemical problem

A composite graphite–silicon electrode contains particles and pores with distinct transport and mechanical environments. Electrode-averaged porosity or capacity can conceal which particles react, deform or recover during a cycle.

What the researchers did

The authors imaged a composite electrode during interrupted galvanostatic formation in a lithium-metal test cell. A random forest in Ilastik classified image phases and silicon-rich cluster regions. Digital volume correlation and separate geometrical analysis then measured particle motion, strain and transport-related features. The high-magnification acquisition used 50 nm voxels.

What they found

Of 66 selected graphite particles, 56 could be followed through the formation cycle (about 85%). Local activity and mechanical recovery varied with particle structure, position and neighboring silicon-rich domains. The researchers identified relatively fast ion-access routes that could counteract the expected through-thickness gradients in electrochemical activity.

Why this matters

Here AI contributes at the measurement-analysis stage: phase segmentation enables the extraction of structural quantities from three-dimensional images. The main discovery arises from combining those quantities with electrochemical cycling, displacement tracking and transport analysis. It is an example of machine learning supporting mechanistic imaging rather than replacing electrochemical interpretation.

One important caution

The study examines the initial formation cycle and a selected imaged region, not an independent sample of 56 cells. Silicon below the effective imaging resolution could not be directly resolved; part of its role was inferred from the neighboring graphite response. The scans interrupted current flow, and the work does not establish long-term aging behavior.
