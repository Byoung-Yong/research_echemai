Research Note 31 · 24 November 2026

Can reactive machine-learning dynamics explain how graphite lithiation changes the initial SEI?

Reactive atomistic simulations examine how the local lithium population at graphite edge sites influences electrolyte decomposition and interphase formation.

Editorial note by EchemAI · Literature window: 6–12 November 2026

In brief

QuestionHow does the lithiation state of graphite affect the first stages of electrolyte reduction?
ResultNanosecond-scale simulations associate low lithium loading with mobile reduction intermediates and high loading with inorganic-rich clusters that inhibit additional decomposition.
Why it mattersMachine learning expands the time window for investigating reactions at a battery interface, not merely equilibrium structures.
Unraveling the Impact of Surface Lithiation on the Initial Solid Electrolyte Interphase Formation on Graphite via Machine Learning Molecular Dynamics
Yu-Hang Tang, Feng Wang, Yimin Wei and Jun Cheng · ACS Nano · Published online 6 November 2026
DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.6c13417

The electrochemical problem

Initial solid electrolyte interphase formation involves chemical reactions, electron transfer and transport near the graphite surface. The short timescales of conventional first-principles dynamics can make the development of an interfacial layer difficult to follow.

What the researchers did

Tang and colleagues trained a reactive machine-learning interatomic potential and used it to simulate solvent reduction over nanosecond timescales. Local lithium loading at graphite edge sites was varied to represent different electrode lithiation conditions associated with changes in potential.

What they found

The modeled reaction environment changed with loading. At lower local lithium content, incomplete reduction allowed intermediates to move away from the interface. At higher loading, inorganic-rich clusters formed at the interface, and the subsequent decomposition of solvent was inhibited in the simulations.

Why this matters

The work illustrates a potential-dependent feedback between the state of an electrode and the chemistry of its developing passivation layer. A reactive learned potential gives access to pathways and transient products that may be difficult to sample with short ab initio trajectories alone.

One important caution

The findings concern simulations of early interphase formation at the studied graphite configurations. Local lithium loading is a model variable associated with potential; the abstract alone does not establish a universal voltage calibration, long-term passivation or experimental agreement. Quantitative training errors and product statistics were not independently checked.

Primary source

Yu-Hang Tang, Feng Wang, Yimin Wei and Jun Cheng. Unraveling the Impact of Surface Lithiation on the Initial Solid Electrolyte Interphase Formation on Graphite via Machine Learning Molecular Dynamics. ACS Nano 20, 30891–30902 (2026). DOI · PubMed.
