Can machine learning turn phase-field simulations into a usable map of lithium dendrite risk?

A phase-field model separates diffusion and electromigration effects on lithium deposition, then Gaussian-process regression maps local electrochemical conditions to Coulombic efficiency.

Editorial note by EchemAI

In brief

ResultThe simulations link higher voltage or current density with stronger dendritic branching, while coordinated increases in diffusion coefficient and ionic conductivity suppress tip growth; Gaussian-process regression maps these conditions to Coulombic efficiency.
Why it mattersThe ML model sits downstream of a mechanistic electrochemical model, turning a multidimensional simulation landscape into a faster screening tool.
Machine Learning-Driven Phase Field Numerical Analysis for a Comprehensive Theoretical Framework of Local Electrochemical Environment and Operating Conditions Effects on Dendrite Evolution Mechanisms
Tengxiao Mu et al. · Nano Letters · Published 16 September 2026
DOI: 10.1021/acs.nanolett.6c03313

The electrochemical problem

Lithium dendrites emerge from coupled ion transport, electric fields, deposition kinetics, and local morphology. A single variable such as current density does not fully describe when the interface becomes unstable.

Phase-field methods can resolve that coupled evolution, but exploring a large parameter space with full simulations is computationally demanding. The question is whether ML can preserve the useful relationships after the mechanistic simulations have been run.

What the researchers did

The study used a phase-field framework to separate the contributions of diffusion and electromigration under different electrochemical operating conditions. It examined how voltage, current density, diffusion coefficient, and ionic conductivity change deposition morphology and stripping behavior.

The simulated data were then used to train a Gaussian-process regression model that maps the local electrochemical environment and operating conditions to Coulombic efficiency.

What they found

Higher voltages or current densities destabilized ion migration and promoted branching in the simulations, even though some conditions improved stripping efficiency. Larger diffusion coefficients supported lateral ion replenishment, while higher ionic conductivity promoted faster and more uniform electromigration.

The combined transport effects suppressed tip growth more effectively than either factor alone. Gaussian-process regression provided a compact predictive mapping from these local conditions to Coulombic efficiency.

Why this matters

This is a useful pattern for electrochemical AI: mechanistic modeling generates physically organized data, and ML converts that data into a fast surrogate for screening.

The result is more interpretable than a direct black-box lifetime predictor because each input retains a clear transport meaning and the phase-field model provides the causal structure behind the trends.

One important caution

The predictive map inherits the assumptions and parameterization of the phase-field model. A high predicted Coulombic efficiency in this computational space is not equivalent to demonstrated long-term performance in a real lithium-metal cell.

Experimental validation across electrolytes, interfaces, current protocols, and cell geometries is still needed before the surrogate can be treated as a general screening rule.
