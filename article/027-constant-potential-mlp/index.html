Research Note 27 · 24 November 2026

Can a machine-learning potential respect the thermodynamics of a constant-potential electrode?

An energy-based model treats electronic exchange with a reservoir consistently and predicts how reaction energetics change with electrode potential.

Editorial note by EchemAI · Literature window: 9–15 October 2026

In brief

QuestionCan an atomistic potential describe an electrified interface while allowing the electronic population to respond to voltage?
ResultThe model, trained on energy and force labels, predicts potential-dependent C–C coupling barriers and charge transfer on Cu(100).
Why it mattersThermodynamic consistency connects the modeled electrochemical driving force to the same energy landscape used for atomic forces.
Thermodynamically Consistent Machine Learning Potentials for Constant-Potential Simulations
Zichen Song, Jiaqi Wang, Jian Han and Lei Li · Physical Review Letters · Published online 15 October 2026
DOI: 10.1103/9xzk-bg72

The electrochemical problem

An electrode held at a controlled potential exchanges electronic charge with an external circuit. A fixed-electron-number molecular simulation does not automatically describe this boundary condition. Predicting a reaction barrier at an assigned voltage requires a consistent description of charge, energy and potential.

What the researchers did

Song and colleagues constructed a constant-potential machine-learning potential based on a single thermodynamic energy function. Its differentiation yields the work function, so the voltage response is linked to the energy model rather than treated as an unrelated prediction task. The reported training targets were energies and atomic forces.

What they found

The demonstration concerns carbon–carbon bond formation at a Cu(100) surface. The model predicts changes in the activation barrier with voltage and describes accompanying charge redistribution. Its formulation includes long-range electrostatics and electronic transfer.

Why this matters

For computational electrochemistry, the question is not just how rapidly a neural potential evaluates forces. It is whether the learned energy representation obeys the relations needed to interpret potential-dependent reactions. This approach provides a route for testing that requirement in accelerated simulations.

One important caution

The accessible publisher abstract does not provide the detailed barrier errors, training-set size or breadth of validation across electrolytes. The note therefore treats this as a methodological demonstration on the reported copper system, rather than proof of universal accuracy or independent experimental agreement.

Primary source

Zichen Song, Jiaqi Wang, Jian Han and Lei Li. Thermodynamically Consistent Machine Learning Potentials for Constant-Potential Simulations. Physical Review Letters 137, 168001 (2026). DOI · Publisher · PubMed.
